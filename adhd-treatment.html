ADHD Treatment & Medication Options

Receiving an ADHD diagnosis can help you better understand your experiences and consider what support may be most helpful for you.

At Rosora Autism & ADHD Assessment Practice, we personalise treatment recommendations. No single approach is right for everyone.

Personalised, never one-size-fits-all

No single approach is right for everyone. Rosora personalises treatment recommendations.

Discussed before it starts

Where ADHD medication is being considered, your clinician will discuss the potential benefits, possible side effects, your preferences and any relevant physical or mental health considerations before treatment is started.

Guided by NICE

Treatment decisions are made in line with relevant NICE guidance (NG87) and appropriate prescribing standards.

Medication

ADHD Medication

Medication can be an effective treatment option for some people with ADHD. It does not cure ADHD, but for some people it can help reduce core ADHD symptoms and improve day-to-day functioning.

NICE recommends different medication pathways for children, young people and adults. Medication is therefore selected individually rather than using a one-size-fits-all approach.

Depending on age, individual circumstances, previous treatment response and clinical suitability, medicines used in the treatment of ADHD may include:

  • Methylphenidate
  • Lisdexamfetamine
  • Dexamfetamine
  • Atomoxetine
  • Guanfacinewhere clinically appropriate for children and young people
Safety first

Before starting ADHD medication

Before prescribing medication, an appropriate clinical assessment is required. This may include consideration of:

Your current ADHD symptoms and how they affect everyday life
Mental health and neurodevelopmental history
Current medication and relevant medical history
Cardiovascular history
Blood pressure and pulse
Height and weight, where appropriate
Potential medication interactions
Risk of substance misuse or medication diversion
Your personal preferences and treatment goals

Your clinician may request additional physical health information or investigations where clinically indicated.

Your clinician will explain the proposed treatment and give you an opportunity to discuss any questions or concerns before you decide whether medication is right for you.

Ongoing care

Medication titration and monitoring

ADHD medication normally requires a period of titration and clinical monitoring.

This means we review treatment carefully to identify an appropriate dose while monitoring effectiveness, tolerability, and any side effects.

Medication will only be continued where it remains clinically appropriate.

Reviews may consider:

Changes in ADHD symptoms
Day-to-day functioning
Side effects
Sleep and appetite
Blood pressure and pulse
Weight and other relevant physical health measures
Emotional and mental wellbeing
Support

Non-medication support

Medication is not the only option for managing ADHD.

Some people choose not to take medication, while others find that a combination of medication and non-medication strategies works best. Depending on individual needs, support may include:

ADHD psychoeducation

Developing a better understanding of ADHD and how it affects you.

Neurodiversity coaching

Developing practical strategies that work with your individual strengths and needs.

Executive-function support

Organisation, planning, prioritising and time management.

Goal setting

Turning larger goals into realistic, manageable steps.

Emotional regulation

Developing strategies for managing strong emotions and overwhelm.

Problem-solving strategies

Sleep and routine support

Wellbeing and lifestyle strategies

Confidence and self-esteem

Workplace or educational strategies

Reasonable adjustments

Accountability and maintaining progress

Rosora takes a person-centred approach. Recommendations are based on your individual needs rather than assuming that every person with ADHD requires the same treatment.

Together

A combined approach

For many people, ADHD support involves more than one intervention.

A personalised treatment plan might therefore combine appropriate medication with psychoeducation, practical strategies, environmental adjustments and neurodiversity-informed coaching.

Our aim is to help you understand the available options so you can make treatment decisions collaboratively and safely.

Appropriate medication+ Psychoeducation+ Practical strategies+ Environmental adjustments+ Neurodiversity-informed coaching
NHS care

NHS care and shared-care agreements

Some people who receive a private ADHD diagnosis may wish to discuss ongoing prescribing with their NHS GP. Where appropriate, Rosora can provide relevant clinical documentation for you and your GP following assessment and treatment.

Not automatic

A private ADHD diagnosis does not automatically guarantee that an NHS GP will take over prescribing or enter into a shared-care agreement.

The GP decides

Shared care is subject to the GP's clinical decision, local NHS policies and the requirements of the relevant NHS commissioning arrangements.

Talk to your GP first

We recommend discussing this with your GP before beginning private medication treatment if transferring prescribing to NHS primary care is important to you.

If your GP says no

Ongoing prescribing and medication monitoring may need to remain with the appropriate private specialist service, with the associated private costs explained to you in advance.

Your choice

Making the right decision for you

Starting ADHD medication is an individual decision.

Our role is to provide clear clinical information, discuss appropriate options and support informed decision-making. You will never be expected to choose medication simply because you have received an ADHD diagnosis.

For some people, medication forms an important part of treatment. For others, non-medication strategies or a combination of approaches may be more appropriate. Your treatment should be based on your needs, preferences and clinical circumstances.
