Access to Work Support

Practical workplace support for neurodivergent people

Work can be rewarding, but the way a workplace is organised does not always match the way a neurodivergent person thinks, processes information, communicates or manages attention.

Enquire About Access to Work Coaching

Support to start and stay in work

If ADHD, Autism, Dyslexia or another health condition affects you at work, Access to Work may be able to provide practical support to help you start work, stay in work and perform your role more effectively.

Personalised coaching

At Rosora, we provide personalised neurodiversity coaching designed to help you understand your working style, develop practical strategies and build greater confidence and independence at work.

Through your award

Where your Access to Work award includes appropriate coaching or specialist support, Rosora may be able to support you with your agreed workplace goals.

The programme

What is Access to Work?

Access to Work is a UK government programme administered by the Department for Work and Pensions (DWP).

It can provide practical and financial support where a disability, neurodevelopmental difference or health condition creates additional barriers at work.

The support available depends on your individual circumstances, your job and the difficulties you experience in carrying out your role.
Access to Work is separate from the reasonable adjustments that an employer may have a legal responsibility to provide.
Eligibility

Can I apply if I have ADHD or Autism?

Yes.

Access to Work can support people with a range of conditions and disabilities, including:

  • ADHD
  • Autism
  • Dyslexia
  • Dyspraxia
  • Mental health conditions
  • Long-term physical health conditions
  • Sensory or physical disabilities
  • Other conditions that affect your ability to work

You usually need to be aged 16 or over and be in paid employment, self-employment, or about to start or return to paid work.

You do not need a formal diagnosis to apply

You do not necessarily need a formal diagnosis to make an Access to Work application.

The important issue is how your condition or difficulties affect you in your job and what support you need to reduce those barriers.

A diagnostic or clinical report may sometimes be useful supporting information, but Access to Work determines what information is required for each application.

Everyone’s experience is different

How neurodivergence can affect work

For some people, ADHD or another neurodevelopmental difference may affect:

  • Planning and prioritising
  • Starting tasks
  • Completing tasks
  • Time awareness
  • Meeting deadlines
  • Working memory
  • Organisation
  • Managing emails and administration
  • Maintaining attention
  • Managing distractions
  • Switching between tasks
  • Emotional regulation
  • Communication
  • Confidence
  • Coping with changes or uncertainty
  • Managing workload
  • Establishing sustainable routines
  • Preventing overwhelm or burnout

You may be highly capable in your role while still finding particular aspects of the working environment disproportionately difficult.

The purpose of workplace support is not to change who you are. It is to identify the barriers you experience and develop practical ways of helping you work more effectively.

The support

What can Access to Work help with?

The support awarded is individually assessed by Access to Work. Depending on your needs and circumstances, support may include areas such as:

01

Specialist workplace support

Support may be available to help you develop strategies for managing difficulties related to your condition within the workplace.

02

Assistive technology

Access to Work may recommend specialist software or equipment where this is considered necessary for your role.

This could include technology that supports organisation, communication, reading, writing or other aspects of workplace functioning.

03

Support workers

Some individuals may qualify for specialist support, such as a job coach or another type of support worker.

04

Travel support

Where a disability or health condition means you cannot use your usual form of transport to travel to work, Access to Work may consider additional travel support.

05

Workplace adaptations

Support may also include certain adaptations or specialist equipment where these are appropriate and are not considered the employer's responsibility as a reasonable adjustment.

Your Access to Work adviser will determine what support can be approved.

Coaching through Rosora

Neurodiversity coaching through Rosora

If coaching or specialist strategy support forms part of your Access to Work award, Rosora can work with you to translate your workplace challenges into practical, achievable strategies.

Our approach is collaborative, strengths-based and neurodiversity-informed. We focus on helping you understand how you work best rather than expecting you to fit into one standard way of working.

Your coaching programme is built around your individual workplace needs and agreed goals.

Workplace coaching

What can Rosora workplace coaching help with?

Depending on your needs, your coaching sessions may focus on:

Organisation and planning

Develop practical systems for managing workload, appointments, tasks, projects and competing priorities.

Time management

Build strategies for time awareness, estimating how long tasks will take, meeting deadlines and creating realistic schedules.

Task initiation

Understand what makes it difficult to begin certain tasks and develop approaches that help you move from intention into action.

Prioritisation

Learn how to distinguish between urgent, important and lower-priority tasks when everything feels equally demanding.

Focus and distraction

Develop strategies for protecting attention, reducing unnecessary interruptions and creating working conditions that support concentration.

Executive functioning

Explore practical ways to support planning, working memory, organisation, task switching and follow-through.

Managing procrastination

Identify patterns behind procrastination and build realistic strategies that reduce avoidance and make tasks easier to approach.

Emotional regulation

Develop greater awareness of emotional triggers and practical approaches to managing frustration, overwhelm, workplace pressure and difficult interactions.

Communication

Explore ways to communicate your needs clearly, prepare for meetings, manage workplace conversations and reduce misunderstandings.

Workplace confidence

Build confidence in recognising your strengths, expressing your needs and making decisions about how you work.

Reasonable adjustments

Coaching can help you identify which aspects of your working environment create barriers and consider adjustments that you may wish to discuss with your employer.

Managing change

Develop strategies for handling changes to workload, responsibilities, routines, management structures or working environments.

Preventing overwhelm and burnout

Recognise early warning signs and develop sustainable ways of managing workload, boundaries, energy and recovery.

The process

Your Rosora workplace coaching plan

Your coaching begins with understanding what is currently happening at work and what you would like to improve. Together, we may work through five stages:

  1. 1

    Understand

    Identify your strengths, workplace challenges and the situations where you experience the greatest difficulty.

  2. 2

    Prioritise

    Decide which areas would make the most meaningful difference to your working life.

  3. 3

    Plan

    Develop realistic goals and practical strategies that fit your role.

  4. 4

    Put strategies into practice

    Experiment with tools, systems and approaches in your real working environment.

  5. 5

    Review and adapt

    Review what is helping, what needs changing and how you can continue using the strategies independently.

The aim is to build sustainable skills rather than create dependence on coaching.

Know the difference

Access to Work and reasonable adjustments are different

Access to Work and reasonable adjustments are related, but they are not the same thing.

Reasonable adjustments

Under the Equality Act 2010, employers may have a legal duty to make reasonable adjustments where a disabled employee would otherwise experience a substantial disadvantage. Examples might include:

  • Providing written as well as verbal instructions
  • Reducing unnecessary distractions
  • Providing a quieter place to work
  • Adjusting working arrangements
  • Allowing appropriate breaks
  • Changing how information is presented
  • Providing additional structure or check-ins

What is considered reasonable depends on the individual circumstances.

Access to Work

Access to Work is a government programme that may provide funding for additional specialist support beyond adjustments that an employer would reasonably be expected to provide. Access to Work will decide what support is eligible for funding.

NICE guidance and workplace support

NICE guidance for ADHD recognises the importance of considering how ADHD affects employment and recommends appropriate environmental modifications to reduce the impact of symptoms.

Environmental modifications can include changes that reduce distractions, improve structure and make information or tasks easier to manage. Rosora’s coaching approach complements this principle by helping individuals identify practical strategies and workplace changes that reflect their individual needs.

How to apply

How to apply for Access to Work

You normally apply directly to Access to Work yourself. The process generally involves:

Go to GOV.UK
  1. Step 1

    Identify your workplace needs

    Think about the parts of your job that are more difficult because of your condition. Try to identify specific examples.

    Instead of

    “I struggle with organisation.”

    You might explain

    “I find it difficult to prioritise several competing tasks, which can result in missed deadlines unless I have a clear system for organising and reviewing my workload.”

    Specific examples can make it easier to explain the support you need.

  2. Step 2

    Apply to Access to Work

    Applications can be made through the official GOV.UK Access to Work service. You may be asked for information about:

    • Your condition or disability
    • How it affects your work
    • Your job
    • Your workplace
    • The type of support you believe may help
    • Your employer or workplace contact
    • Your employment or self-employment details
  3. Step 3

    Discuss your needs

    An Access to Work adviser may contact you to discuss your circumstances and the difficulties you experience at work. Some applicants may also require a workplace assessment.

  4. Step 4

    Receive your decision

    Access to Work will decide what support has been approved and provide details of the award.

  5. Step 5

    Arrange approved support

    Once you have received your award, you can arrange the support in accordance with the terms of your Access to Work agreement.

Get ready

Preparing for your Access to Work conversation

It can help to think about your needs before speaking with an Access to Work adviser. Consider:

Focus on your functional needs, rather than feeling that you need to prove how severe your diagnosis is.

Which parts of your job are most difficult?
What happens when those difficulties occur?
How often do they happen?
How do they affect your productivity or wellbeing?
What have you already tried?
What helps?
What does not help?
Are there particular environments or situations where things become harder?
Would structured specialist support help you develop sustainable strategies?
Working for yourself

Access to Work if you are self-employed

Self-employed people may also be eligible for Access to Work, subject to the programme’s eligibility requirements. Support may be particularly useful where neurodivergence affects areas such as:

Check the current eligibility requirements directly with Access to Work before applying.

  • Managing workload
  • Business administration
  • Planning
  • Scheduling
  • Prioritisation
  • Invoicing and paperwork
  • Client communication
  • Maintaining routines
  • Separating work and personal time
  • Managing multiple projects

Do I need to tell my employer?

You do not have to disclose every aspect of your medical history to your employer.

However, where workplace support needs to be arranged, Access to Work may need to involve your employer or workplace contact, with the appropriate permissions.

If you are unsure about disclosure, Rosora coaching can help you think through how you would like to communicate your needs professionally and confidently.

What Access to Work does not replace

Access to Work is workplace support. It does not replace:

  • A clinical ADHD or Autism assessment
  • Medical treatment
  • ADHD medication
  • Psychological therapy
  • Your employer's responsibility to consider reasonable adjustments

If you are looking for an Autism or ADHD assessment, this should be considered separately from an Access to Work application. See our adult assessments.

Coaching focused on everyday working life

Rosora provides neurodiversity-informed coaching focused on the practical challenges that can arise in everyday working life. We can help you:

  • Understand your working style
  • Identify strengths and barriers
  • Build executive-function strategies
  • Improve organisation and planning
  • Develop sustainable routines
  • Manage competing priorities
  • Work on procrastination and task initiation
  • Strengthen workplace communication
  • Develop confidence
  • Explore reasonable adjustments
  • Improve work-life balance
  • Reduce overwhelm
  • Build strategies you can continue using independently

Our aim is not to tell you how you should work. It is to help you discover what helps you work at your best.
