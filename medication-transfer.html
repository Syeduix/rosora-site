Treatment & Medication

Already diagnosed with ADHD? Transfer your medication care to Rosora

If you are 18 or over and already have an ADHD diagnosis from another provider, Rosora may be able to support you with ongoing medication treatment, titration and clinical monitoring.

Why we review first

Before we take over your care, we first need to review your existing diagnostic assessment and relevant medical information. This helps us confirm that we have enough clinical evidence to provide treatment safely and that your previous assessment meets the standards required within the Rosora service.

No need to start again

Already diagnosed does not mean you need to start your assessment again. Where your existing diagnostic evidence is sufficient, you can move directly into our medication transfer pathway.

Medication is never automatic

Medication is not automatically prescribed simply because a previous ADHD diagnosis has been accepted. Your clinician decides whether it is clinically appropriate.

The Process

How the Rosora transfer of care process works

Six clear steps, from submitting your documents to stable treatment and ongoing care.

Start Your Transfer of Care
  1. Step 1

    Submit your documents

    Upload your existing ADHD diagnostic report and relevant clinical information securely through the Rosora patient portal.

    We may ask you to provide:

    • Your full ADHD diagnostic report
    • Recent clinic letters, where available
    • Current or previous ADHD medication information
    • Current medication list
    • Relevant medical history
    • Recent blood pressure and pulse readings
    • Height and weight where clinically appropriate
    • Relevant GP or specialist correspondence

    Secure upload. No live appointment is required at this stage.

  2. Step 2£50

    Clinical document review

    A Rosora clinician reviews the information you have provided. The purpose of this review is to determine whether:

    • Your previous ADHD assessment contains sufficient diagnostic evidence
    • The diagnosis is adequately documented
    • Appropriate diagnostic criteria were considered
    • Relevant developmental history and functional impairment were assessed
    • Differential diagnoses and coexisting conditions were appropriately considered
    • There is sufficient information for Rosora to safely consider taking over medication treatment
    Document review fee£50

    This is a clinical records review and does not guarantee acceptance for medication treatment. Following the review, we will explain the appropriate next step.

    Book Document Review
  3. Step 3

    Receive your clinical review outcome

    Once your documents have been reviewed, we will contact you with the outcome. There are usually three possible pathways:

    Pathway A — Accepted for transfer of careYour previous diagnostic assessment provides sufficient evidence and you will be invited to book an Initial Transfer of Care Medication Appointment.
    Pathway B — Further information requiredWe may ask for additional reports, questionnaires, medical information or clarification before a decision can be made.
    Pathway C — Further ADHD assessment recommendedIf your previous report does not contain enough information for Rosora to safely rely upon the diagnosis, we may recommend additional assessment or a new ADHD diagnostic assessment.

    We will explain why further assessment is required before you decide whether to proceed.

  4. Step 4£295

    Initial transfer of care medication appointment

    Meet with your prescribing clinician — Adults 18+

    If your diagnostic review is accepted, you will be invited to attend a comprehensive medication appointment with an appropriate prescribing clinician.

    Recommended Rosora fee£295

    During this appointment, your clinician will review:

    • Your ADHD diagnosis
    • Current ADHD symptoms
    • Functional difficulties
    • Previous ADHD treatment
    • Current or previous medication
    • Medication response and side effects
    • Medical history
    • Mental health history
    • Current medication and potential interactions
    • Relevant cardiovascular history
    • Blood pressure and pulse
    • Height and weight where appropriate
    • Your treatment preferences
    • Your treatment goals

    Your clinician will discuss the available treatment options with you and determine whether ADHD medication is clinically appropriate. Where medication is suitable, an individual treatment plan will be agreed.

    Book Transfer of Care Appointment

    Medication is not automatically prescribed simply because a previous ADHD diagnosis has been accepted.

  5. Step 5£150

    Medication titration & follow-up

    If you are starting ADHD medication, restarting treatment or require a medication adjustment, you may enter the Rosora titration pathway.

    Titration is the process of carefully reviewing treatment over time to identify an appropriate medication and dose while monitoring effectiveness and tolerability.

    Medication follow-up review — recommended Rosora fee£150

    per review

    Reviews will normally consider:

    • ADHD symptom response
    • Day-to-day functioning
    • Medication effectiveness
    • Side effects
    • Sleep
    • Appetite
    • Emotional wellbeing
    • Blood pressure and pulse
    • Weight where clinically appropriate
    • Treatment adherence
    • Any changes in physical or mental health

    Medication may be adjusted where clinically appropriate.

    The number and frequency of appointments will depend on your individual treatment response and clinical needs. Some people require several titration reviews before treatment becomes stable.

  6. Step 6

    Stable medication & ongoing care

    Once your medication and dose are considered stable, we will discuss your longer-term treatment arrangements. This may include:

    • Continuing private prescribing through Rosora
    • Scheduled medication monitoring
    • Specialist medication reviews
    • Annual medication review
    • Communication with your GP
    • Consideration of shared care where appropriate
Already stable?

Already stable on ADHD medication?

If you are already taking ADHD medication and wish to transfer ongoing treatment to Rosora, we still need to complete the document review and clinical transfer appointment.

Your clinician will review:

  • Your diagnosis
  • Current treatment
  • Medication response
  • Side effects
  • Current physical-health monitoring
  • Whether your current treatment remains clinically appropriate

If treatment is stable and Rosora is able to accept your care, you may not require a full titration pathway. You will still require appropriate ongoing medication monitoring.

If the answer is not yet

What if my previous ADHD assessment is not accepted?

Rosora has a responsibility to make sure there is sufficient clinical evidence before taking responsibility for ADHD medication treatment.

This does not automatically mean your previous diagnosis is incorrect. It means Rosora requires enough evidence to safely take responsibility for your ongoing treatment.

Where additional assessment is recommended, we will explain the reasons and any associated costs before you decide whether to proceed.

We may recommend further assessment where:

  • The diagnostic report is incomplete
  • Important developmental information is missing
  • Functional impairment is not adequately documented
  • Diagnostic criteria are unclear
  • Differential diagnoses have not been sufficiently considered
  • There are significant changes in your clinical presentation
  • The information provided creates uncertainty about the diagnosis
  • Additional clinical information is required for safe treatment

Private prescriptions & medication costs

The cost of ADHD medication is separate from your Rosora appointment fee. Medication costs are set by the dispensing pharmacy and can vary depending on:

  • Medication
  • Strength
  • Formulation
  • Brand
  • Availability
  • Pharmacy charges
  • Delivery arrangements

Any Rosora private prescription or administration fee will be clearly explained before treatment begins.

Shared care with your NHS GP

Once treatment is stable, your clinician may consider whether it is appropriate to approach your GP regarding a shared-care arrangement. Where appropriate, Rosora can provide relevant clinical documentation to support this request.

However, shared care cannot be guaranteed.

Your GP remains responsible for deciding whether they are willing to enter into a shared-care arrangement, taking account of local NHS policies and prescribing arrangements.

If your GP does not accept shared care, you may continue treatment privately through Rosora where clinically appropriate.
